Don't Buy Your First Futures Trading Computer Until You Read This

Your first futures trading computer does not need to be impressive. It needs to be dependable. What you spent on your machine won't matter if it locks up while you're in a trade.

In this post I'm going to show you exactly what to look for: laptop or desktop, Windows or Mac, CPU, RAM, storage, graphics card, how many monitors, and the internet setup almost everyone ignores. You'll walk away knowing where to spend and where you're just buying a trophy.

When I bought my first machine I had no idea what I was doing, spent money on the wrong specs, and had to replace it sooner than I should have.

Prefer to watch instead? This post comes from the video below.

Should you buy a laptop or a desktop for futures trading?

Desktops win on paper. More performance per dollar, better cooling, easier to upgrade, longer useful life. If you already know you're going to run four charts on four screens from one spot, a desktop is the better machine.

But they don't move. And when you're starting out, being near the charts matters more than raw horsepower.

Early on, portability mattered to me way more than I expected. I could sit with the charts in the living room, at the kitchen table, on the road. Screen time is how you build a feel for a market, and a machine you can carry gets you more of it.

Recommendation: if you're new, buy the laptop. You can add monitors later. You can't add mobility later.

If you do go desktop, get remote access software set up on day one so you can at least monitor from your phone or tablet when you're away. I use TeamViewer or Chrome Remote Desktop. Both are easy to set up and allow you to see all your monitors at once or individually.

Windows or Mac?

Most futures platforms are built for Windows. I use NinjaTrader, which is Windows native.

Can you trade on a Mac? Yes. Plenty of people do, running Windows through Parallels or Boot Camp. It works. It's also an extra layer of things that can break on a morning you don't have time for things to break.

Here's my rule: you want your setup to be boring. Ease of execution is the whole game. Don't build a barrier between you and your order before you've placed your first one.

If you're committed to Mac, commit from the start. Don't switch platforms halfway through your learning curve.

CPU: this is the one to spend on

The processor handles your chart calculations, your indicators, your incoming market data. It runs everything.

More cores is not automatically better. A current-generation chip beats an older chip with a bigger core count almost every time. An Intel i5 or i7, or an AMD Ryzen 5 or 7, is plenty for charting. I run an i9, which is more than I need.

What to avoid:

  • Very old processors. You'll outgrow them fast.
  • Ultra-budget chips. They're cheap for a reason.
  • Refurbished, for your primary machine.

If you only get to pay up on one thing, pay up here.

How much RAM do you need for a futures trading computer?

Sixteen gigabytes is the floor. Thirty-two is ideal. Sixty-four is probably unnecessary but guaranteed to run well.

This is where I blew it. My first machine had 8GB and it did not hold up. NinjaTrader on its own is not especially demanding. But you're never running NinjaTrader on its own. You've got a browser with nine tabs, a Discord, a news feed, a spreadsheet, maybe a second charting site. That's where the freezing and the endless loading come from.

Which brings up the other half of this: dedicate the machine to trading. No personal stuff. No gaming, no photo library, no random software you installed once. Use a different device for things like reading this sweet blog.

Storage: SSD, no exceptions

It would be hard to find at this point, but never buy a trading computer with a traditional spinning hard drive. Solid state, every time. Faster boots, faster chart loads, programs open instantly, and fewer mechanical parts to fail.

512GB works fine. A terabyte gives you breathing room for data and market replay files.

Prioritize the type of drive over the size of the drive. A 512GB SSD beats a 2TB spinner for what we do, all day long.

Graphics card: respect the minimum, ignore the flex

This is where beginners either overspend wildly or underspend and regret it in a year.

Charts are not video games. You do not need a high-end gaming GPU to look at candles. The visual upgrade is not worth the money at this stage.

What you do need is enough graphics capability to drive at least two external monitors when you're ready for them. That was the second mistake I made with my first trading computer: my machine could only handle one external screen, so I hit a wall earlier than I wanted to.

You need a genuinely capable dedicated card when you're running four to six monitors.

How many monitors do you need to trade futures?

One is fine. Two is the sweet spot, usually after a year or so. Three is great. Past that you're optimizing workflow, not profitability.

I run four because my process puts a tick chart, a 60-minute chart, a daily, and a volatility read in front of me at all times. That's a system I grew into over years, and you can trace the evolution of my setup if you're curious what each stage looked like. But more screens can also become more distraction early on. Trading is hard enough. Don't add noise.

Internet: stable beats fast

Your connection must be stable, not blazing fast. Those are different things.

  • Wire it if you can. Ethernet over Wi-Fi, always.
  • Latency matters more than download speed.
  • Have a backup. A phone hotspot counts.

An unstable connection can cost you real money at the worst possible moment. Consistent beats fast, in your internet and in your trading.

New or refurbished?

Refurbished can save you a chunk of money, and business-class refurbs are often professionally restored. The tradeoff is older hardware and a shorter warranty.

I'm not going to spend long here, because my answer is short. For your primary trading machine, buy new.

The part the hardware can't fix

A faster computer does not make you a better trader. It removes friction.

It's the same honest line I use about the NinjaTrader add-ons I build: they make your plan faster to execute, they cannot make a bad plan good.

Quick recap

  1. Laptop if you're new. Desktop if you're staying put.
  2. Windows unless you have a strong reason not to.
  3. CPU first. Current generation, mid-tier or better.
  4. 16GB RAM minimum, 32GB ideal.
  5. SSD, 512GB or more.
  6. Graphics that supports two external monitors, minimum.
  7. One monitor to start. Add screens when you can name what goes on them.
  8. Wired internet plus a hotspot backup.
  9. Buy new.

Your first futures trading computer does not need to be impressive. It needs to be dependable. Put the money you save toward education, screen time, and reps.

FAQ

Can you trade futures on a laptop?

Yes, and for most beginners it's the better choice. A laptop with a current i5 or i7, 16GB of RAM, and an SSD handles NinjaTrader comfortably. Just get a wireless mouse. Trading from a trackpad is genuinely awful.

Is 8GB of RAM enough for NinjaTrader?

Technically it will run, but it's not enough in practice. Once you add a browser, a news feed, and a chat app, you'll hit slowdowns and freezing. Sixteen gigabytes is the realistic minimum in 2026, and 32GB gives you room to grow.

Do I need a gaming PC to trade futures?

No. Charting does not stress a graphics card the way games do. What you actually need is enough graphics capability to run at least two external monitors later, which most mid-range machines already have.

How many monitors do professional futures traders use?

It varies a lot, and it says nothing about skill. Two is a common working setup, three is comfortable, and more than that is usually about workflow preference. Start with one and add a screen only when you can name exactly what would go on it.

Should I buy a refurbished computer for trading?

For a backup machine, sure. For your main trading computer, buy new. The savings aren't worth a shorter warranty and older hardware on the one device you need to be reliable when you're in a position.
